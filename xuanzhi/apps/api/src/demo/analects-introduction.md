# The Analects (论语, Lúnyǔ) — Introduction and Core Teachings

> Demonstration reference content prepared for 玄知 AI. Book and chapter numbers follow the standard twenty-book division (e.g. 1.1 = Book 1, passage 1).

## The text and its author

The *Analects* is a collection of sayings and short dialogues of Confucius (孔子, Kǒngzǐ, 551–479 BCE) compiled by his disciples and their students over several generations after his death. It has twenty books (篇) of loosely grouped passages. Together with the *Mencius* (孟子), the *Great Learning* (大学) and the *Doctrine of the Mean* (中庸) it forms the Four Books (四书) that Zhu Xi (朱熹, 1130–1200) placed at the centre of Confucian education, and which served as the basis of the imperial examinations from 1313 to 1905.

## Core concepts

- **Ren (仁)** — humaneness, benevolence, the fullest human virtue. When asked about ren, Confucius answered 「爱人」 ("love people", 12.22) and, in another passage, 「己所不欲，勿施于人」 ("Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire", 12.2 and 15.24), often called the Confucian golden rule.
- **Li (礼)** — ritual propriety: the norms of ceremony and everyday courtesy that give ren a form. 「克己复礼为仁」 ("To restrain oneself and return to ritual is humaneness", 12.1).
- **Yi (义)** — rightness, doing what is appropriate regardless of profit. 「君子喻于义，小人喻于利」 ("The noble person understands rightness; the petty person understands profit", 4.16).
- **Xiao (孝)** — filial devotion, respect and care for parents, considered the root of ren (1.2).
- **Junzi (君子)** — the "noble person" or exemplary person, defined by character rather than birth.
- **Zhengming (正名)** — the rectification of names: 「君君，臣臣，父父，子子」 ("Let the ruler be a ruler, the minister a minister, the father a father, the son a son", 12.11).
- **Learning (学)** — the opening line 「学而时习之，不亦说乎」 ("To learn and to practise at the proper time — is this not a pleasure?", 1.1) sets the tone of the whole work.

## Famous passages

- **2.4 — Stages of life.** "At fifteen I set my heart on learning; at thirty I stood firm; at forty I had no doubts; at fifty I knew the Mandate of Heaven; at sixty my ear was attuned; at seventy I could follow my heart's desire without overstepping the line."
- **2.11 — Reviewing the old.** 「温故而知新，可以为师矣」: "One who reviews the old and knows the new can be a teacher."
- **2.17 — Knowing.** 「知之为知之，不知为不知，是知也」: "To know that you know, and to know that you do not know — this is knowledge."
- **7.22 — Learning from everyone.** 「三人行，必有我师焉」: "When three people walk together, one of them can surely be my teacher."
- **15.24 — Reciprocity (恕).** Asked for one word to guide a lifetime, Confucius answered "shu (恕)": do not do to others what you would not want done to you.
- **4.8** 「朝闻道，夕死可矣」: "If I hear the Way in the morning, I could die content in the evening."

## Government and society

Confucius argued that rulers should lead by moral example (德) and ritual rather than by punishment alone: 「道之以德，齐之以礼，有耻且格」 ("Guide them by virtue and regulate them by ritual, and they will have a sense of shame and correct themselves", 2.3). The family is the model of the state; harmony arises when every role is performed well.

## Historical layers and interpretation

- **Classical teaching:** the sayings themselves, reflecting Spring-and-Autumn-period values.
- **Traditional interpretation:** Han commentaries, and later Song Neo-Confucian readings (Zhu Xi) that framed the text within a metaphysics of principle (理) and vital force (气).
- **Modern interpretation:** debates on whether Confucianism supports or hinders democracy, gender equality and modern business ethics; "Confucian values" are often invoked in East Asian corporate culture.
- **Evidence-based note:** the text is a compilation with layers of different dates; scholars such as E. Bruce Brooks have proposed that Books 4–8 form the oldest core. Attributions of individual sayings to Confucius himself cannot be verified.
