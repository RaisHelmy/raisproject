# Chinese Classical Wisdom — Proverbs and Life Guidance

> Demonstration reference content prepared for 玄知 AI. Each proverb (成语 chéngyǔ / 谚语 yànyǔ) is given with source, literal meaning and traditional use.

## Family and relationships

- **家和万事兴** (jiā hé wàn shì xīng) — "When the family is harmonious, all things prosper." A folk saying reflecting the Confucian idea that order begins at home (*Great Learning* 大学: 齐家 "regulate the family" precedes 治国 "govern the state").
- **百善孝为先** (bǎi shàn xiào wéi xiān) — "Of the hundred virtues, filial devotion comes first." From the Qing-era *Wei Lu Ye Hua* (围炉夜话) by Wang Yongbin (王永彬). Used to teach that care for parents is the root of character.
- **远亲不如近邻** (yuǎn qīn bù rú jìn lín) — "A distant relative is not as good as a near neighbour." Practical wisdom valuing community over blood ties in daily need.

## Learning and self-cultivation

- **学而不思则罔，思而不学则殆** (*Analects* 2.15) — "Learning without thinking is useless; thinking without learning is dangerous."
- **三人行，必有我师** (*Analects* 7.22) — Everyone has something to teach us.
- **活到老，学到老** (huó dào lǎo, xué dào lǎo) — "Live until old, learn until old." Lifelong learning.
- **玉不琢，不成器** (yù bù zhuó, bù chéng qì) — "Jade that is not carved cannot become a vessel" (*Book of Rites* 礼记·学记). Talent requires training.
- **千里之行，始于足下** (*Dao De Jing* 64) — great undertakings start with a single step.

## Conduct, patience and timing

- **小不忍则乱大谋** (*Analects* 15.27) — "Lack of forbearance in small matters upsets great plans."
- **塞翁失马，焉知非福** (sài wēng shī mǎ, yān zhī fēi fú) — "The old man at the frontier lost his horse; who knows if it is not a blessing?" From the *Huainanzi* (淮南子·人间训). Fortune and misfortune turn into each other; do not judge events too quickly.
- **欲速则不达** (*Analects* 13.17) — "Haste does not bring success."
- **水滴石穿** (shuǐ dī shí chuān) — "Dripping water bores through stone." Persistence over force.
- **知足常乐** (zhī zú cháng lè) — "Contentment brings lasting joy", echoing *Dao De Jing* 44 and 46.

## Ethics and integrity

- **己所不欲，勿施于人** (*Analects* 12.2) — the golden rule of reciprocity.
- **君子坦荡荡，小人长戚戚** (*Analects* 7.37) — "The noble person is open and at ease; the petty person is full of worry."
- **满招损，谦受益** (*Book of Documents* 尚书·大禹谟) — "Pride invites loss; humility receives benefit."
- **善有善报，恶有恶报** (shàn yǒu shàn bào, è yǒu è bào) — "Good is repaid with good, evil with evil." A popular expression of the Buddhist idea of karma (业) as absorbed into Chinese folk ethics.

## Work and prosperity

- **一分耕耘，一分收获** (yī fēn gēng yún, yī fēn shōu huò) — "One part ploughing, one part harvest." Effort and reward are proportional.
- **和气生财** (hé qì shēng cái) — "Harmony brings wealth." A merchant's maxim advising courtesy in business.
- **未雨绸缪** (wèi yǔ chóu móu) — "Repair the roof before it rains" (*Book of Odes* 诗经·豳风·鸱鸮). Prepare in advance.
- **天时、地利、人和** (*Mencius* 孟子·公孙丑下) — "Heaven's timing, Earth's advantages, human harmony": the three conditions of success, of which human harmony is the most important.

## How to use proverbs well

Proverbs compress experience, but they are guidance rather than rules. Classical sources often contain contrasting sayings (for example "haste does not bring success" and "seize the moment" 机不可失), and the skill lies in judging which applies to the present situation — the same emphasis on timing found in the *I Ching*. For decisions involving money, law or health, treat traditional wisdom as perspective and consult qualified professionals for the specifics.
