# The Five Elements (五行, Wǔxíng) — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water

> Demonstration reference content prepared for 玄知 AI. "Five phases" is a more accurate translation than "elements": they are processes, not substances.

## Origins

The theory of the five phases appears in the *Book of Documents* (尚书·洪范) and was systematised in the Warring States and Han periods, notably by Zou Yan (邹衍) and in the *Huainanzi* (淮南子) and the medical classic *Huangdi Neijing* (黄帝内经). It became a universal classification scheme linking seasons, directions, colours, organs, emotions, planets and dynasties.

## The two main cycles

**Generating cycle (相生, xiāngshēng)** — each phase produces the next: Wood feeds Fire; Fire creates Earth (ash); Earth bears Metal; Metal collects Water (condensation); Water nourishes Wood.

**Controlling cycle (相克, xiāngkè)** — each phase restrains another: Wood parts Earth (roots); Earth dams Water; Water quenches Fire; Fire melts Metal; Metal cuts Wood.

Two further relationships describe imbalance: **over-acting (相乘)**, when the controlling phase is excessive, and **insulting (相侮)**, when the controlled phase rebels against its controller. Traditional medicine uses these patterns to describe how one organ system may affect another.

## Use in Ba Zi (八字) and the sexagenary cycle

The ten Heavenly Stems (天干) are each assigned a phase and a yin/yang polarity; the twelve Earthly Branches (地支) carry "hidden stems" and are linked to the twelve animals. A Ba Zi ("Four Pillars", 四柱) chart maps the stem and branch of a person's birth year, month, day and hour, giving eight characters. Practitioners read the balance of the five phases relative to the "Day Master" (日主, the day stem) to describe temperament and favourable influences. This is a traditional interpretive system; there is no scientific evidence that birth time determines personality or fortune.

## Use in Feng Shui

Feng Shui uses the five phases to harmonise materials, colours and shapes in a space. For example, a room associated with Fire (south) may be balanced with Earth or Wood features according to the generating cycle, while water features are traditionally avoided where they would "control" a needed Fire quality. Shapes are also classified: Wood is tall and rectangular, Fire pointed, Earth flat and square, Metal round, Water irregular or wavy.

## Use in Chinese medicine

The *Huangdi Neijing* maps the five phases onto the five zang organs and their paired fu organs, the sense organs, tissues, flavours (sour, bitter, sweet, pungent, salty) and emotions. A traditional physician may say that "Liver Wood is overacting on Spleen Earth" to describe digestive complaints accompanied by irritability. This is the historical explanatory language of traditional Chinese medicine; modern medicine explains such conditions differently, and anyone with a health concern should consult a qualified clinician.

## Framing

- **Classical teaching:** a correlative cosmology organising nature into five interacting processes.
- **Traditional belief:** applied in medicine, divination, Feng Shui and dynastic legitimacy.
- **Modern interpretation:** a systems-thinking metaphor for balance and feedback.
- **Evidence-based view:** the correspondences are cultural and symbolic, not empirically established mechanisms.
