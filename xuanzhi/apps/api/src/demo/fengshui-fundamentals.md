# Feng Shui (风水) — Fundamentals of the Form and Compass Schools

> Demonstration reference content prepared for 玄知 AI. Feng Shui is presented here as a traditional practice, not as a science.

## Meaning and origins

Feng Shui literally means "wind and water". The classic definition comes from the *Book of Burial* (葬书) attributed to Guo Pu (郭璞, 276–324): 「气乘风则散，界水则止」 ("Qi rides the wind and scatters, but is retained when it meets water"). The practice grew out of siting graves and settlements in the landscape so that they would be sheltered from wind and close to water. Over centuries it developed into two broad approaches:

1. **Form School (形势派, xíngshì pài)** — reads the shapes of mountains, rivers, roads and buildings.
2. **Compass School (理气派, lǐqì pài)** — uses the luopan (罗盘) compass, the trigrams, the five phases and time cycles to calculate favourable directions and periods.

Most practitioners combine both.

## Qi (气) and the ideal site

Qi is the traditional term for vital energy or the animating quality of a place. The Form School's ideal site, the "armchair" configuration, has:

- a protective hill behind (**Black Tortoise**, 玄武),
- a lower rise to the left (**Azure Dragon**, 青龙) and a slightly lower rise to the right (**White Tiger**, 白虎),
- an open view and gentle water in front (**Red Bird**, 朱雀), with a small mound or "table mountain" (案山) in the middle distance.

Sheng qi (生气, "living qi") gathers where the land is embraced and water meanders slowly; sha qi (煞气, "harmful qi") is associated with straight roads or ridges pointing at a site, sharp corners, and fast straight water.

## Residential Feng Shui (阳宅, yángzhái)

The classic *Ten Books of Residences* (阳宅十书, Ming dynasty) and the *Eight Mansions* method (八宅) organise the home around three key features:

- **Door (门)** — the "mouth of qi"; it should open onto a bright, clear space and not face a long straight corridor or staircase directly.
- **Stove (灶)** — traditionally represents nourishment and prosperity; it should be stable and not directly opposite the sink or door.
- **Bedroom/bed (床)** — the bed should have a solid wall behind the head, not sit under a beam, and not be in line with the door ("coffin position").

In the Eight Mansions method a house is assigned a trigram from the direction its back faces, giving four auspicious and four inauspicious sectors; occupants are assigned a "Gua number" (命卦) from birth year and sex and are advised to favour their auspicious directions for the bed, desk and stove.

## Compass School methods

- **Luopan (罗盘):** a magnetic compass surrounded by rings showing the 24 Mountains (二十四山), trigrams, stems, branches and star tables.
- **Flying Stars (玄空飞星, Xuánkōng Fēixīng):** combines the facing direction of a building with the construction period (nine 20-year periods forming a 180-year cycle) to place nine "stars" in a grid; stars change with time, which is why practitioners speak of annual afflictions and remedies.
- **Later Heaven Bagua (后天八卦):** maps the eight trigrams to the compass directions (Li/Fire south, Kan/Water north, Zhen/Wood east, Dui/Metal west, etc.), used to associate areas of a home with life aspects in popular modern schools.

## Five phases in practice

The five phases (五行) guide remedies: metal objects are said to weaken an Earth star, water features to feed Wood, and so on. Colours, materials and shapes are chosen to support or restrain the qualities the practitioner wants to adjust.

## Honest framing

- **Classical teaching:** landscape siting principles recorded in texts such as the *Book of Burial* and *Ten Books of Residences*.
- **Traditional belief:** that the arrangement of a space influences health, harmony and fortune.
- **Modern interpretation:** many recommendations overlap with sensible design (light, ventilation, privacy, sightlines, avoiding clutter) and with environmental psychology.
- **Evidence-based view:** there is no scientific evidence that qi flows or that arrangements alter luck. Statements such as "this arrangement is sometimes interpreted as unfavourable in traditional practice" are appropriate; "your house has bad energy" is not. Feng Shui advice should never replace professional advice on building safety, health or finance.
